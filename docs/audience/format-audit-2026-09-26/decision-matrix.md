# Carousel versus reel: decision matrix

Companion to the [executive report](README.md). Each row is an editorial
approach, judged on what the reader needs to do with it, then on our own
evidence. Figures are lifetime views at collection (2026-09-26), ages 1–21
days; row ids refer to [`publications.csv`](publications.csv).

**Revised 2026-09-29 (version 2).** Corrections to version 1: the Instagram
saves comparison points to carousels being lower, not equal; a chronology
carousel does exist (Daloa); "Shorts only" and "carousel as companion" were
prescriptions the sample cannot carry and are now stated as observations; the
myth and source rows no longer restate a review policy. Formats here are
editorial affordances, not performance guarantees: both can teach, attract
discovery and be saved. Nothing was re-collected.

**How to read the "evidence" column.** _Observed_ is a measured result on a
named publication. _Suggestive_ means the pattern appears but subject, date or
network is not controlled. _None_ means we published nothing comparable.
Nothing below is a rule; each row ends in the uncertainty that would change it.

## What our evidence supports, network by network

These are dated observations about our own accounts, written so that they can
be overturned. None is a rule, a ban or a required pairing.

1. **Instagram.** Reels reached more people than carousels in the sample:
   median 1,274 views (n=27) against 141 (n=9), and one same-day pair
   (swahili) is 31×. Eight of the nine carousels were posted on 11 and 12 Sep,
   and 21 more carousels are cross-posts whose Instagram share cannot be
   isolated. Reels are the better-observed way to reach new people here; a
   carousel is a legitimate edition whose reach we have barely measured.
2. **Facebook.** Our 11 multi-photo posts drew 3–30 views, all from 11–14 Sep
   on a page that then grew on reels, and all three results above 60K were
   reels. This describes our page in its first ten days, not the platform.
3. **TikTok.** The medians overlap (354 v 302.5) and both formats produced
   breakouts; the five largest posts are carousels and the best video has
   1,358. Same-day pairs go both ways. Choose the format by the reading or
   viewing task, and judge it against posts of the same age.
4. **YouTube.** Shorts are the best-observed YouTube video format: 30 Shorts,
   median 532. The single standard video (5 views) and seven community image
   posts (5–21 views) are too few, and too far from the subscriber feed, to
   rule any format out. Use community posts as a pointer until tested.
5. **LinkedIn and X did not test formats.** The 12 LinkedIn items with
   metrics have a median of 2 views; the best (a 22 Sep introduction) has 51
   (124 impressions). X has 0 followers. No format conclusion is possible.

## Decision questions to ask before choosing

- _What must the reader do with it?_ Compare, check, save, revisit → a
  carousel gives the reader the pace. Follow a sequence, hear, be carried →
  a reel gives a directed one. Both can teach and both are saved.
- _Is the claim disputed by the people it names?_ Then the piece needs the
  competing accounts named by who holds them, at their own tier, whichever
  format carries it. Which checks apply is decided by the claims and media
  actually in the piece, under the review policy the production chain
  publishes; this matrix does not restate it.
- _Which network is it for?_ The same video was 12.6× the Instagram median and
  0.4× the Facebook median (Keïta): choose subject × network, not format alone.
- _Is this an adaptation, a deepening or a republication?_ All three are
  legitimate and none is required. An adaptation may keep the same angle. State
  which one it is, so the result is read against the right comparison.
