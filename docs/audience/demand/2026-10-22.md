### Observations

1. **Arabic-origin names slip into the "African" lists.** Sekou (Cheikh), Djeneba
   (Zaynab), Lamine (al-Amin), Seydou (Sayyid), Lassana (al-Hassan), Tidjane
   (Tijani), Djibril (Jibril). Commenters cannot tell them apart, which supports
   the site's angle: a name has an origin, then a history in the people who carry
   it.
2. **Commenters correct each other, correctly:** Adama from Adam, Aminata from
   Amina, Oumou from _umm_ ("mother"), Binta from _bint_ ("girl"), Moussa from
   Moses. Contested: Sadjo from Saïd, denied by the bearer.
3. **Folk etymologies circulate with no source we know of:** Aïssatou from Isis;
   Mamadou from an Egyptian "Maa medou"; Mory from "Maure", hence "Mauritania =
   land of the Black people". A sourced atlas is what closes these.
4. **First names and family names are mixed up.** Sow, Bamba, Keita, Koné,
   Samaké, Diomandé, Sylla, Moussavou are mostly patronyms; one reply says
   Kalunji, Kanku and Mbuyi are family or twin names. The corpus already covers
   patronyms: the natural bridge to the site.
5. **Recurring naming systems, each a candidate piece:** birth-day names in
   Ghana, Togo and Benin (Koffi, Kodjo, Kouamé, Komlan, Codjo, Afi); twin names
   (Nsimba, Nzuzi); protective names such as Wolof Kenbougoul, said to mean
   "nobody wants it" (to verify).
6. **The reel's thesis is disputed from inside the thread.** Comments from
   Benin, Madagascar, East and Southern Africa say they keep their names; Amazigh
   comments recall that Africa does not mean Black; one draws the Turkish analogy
   (Mehmet from Mohamed, yet Turkish). Congolese Christian virtue names are cited
   (Victoire, Miracle, Bénédiction) next to their Lingala counterparts, Kimia
   ("peace") and Elikia ("hope").
7. **The thread is tense:** anti-Islam calls, accusations of islamophobia
   against the creator, personal attacks. Any brand reply stays on one name and
   its history, never on religion.

The site has no given-name surface; doctrine covers peoples, languages, families,
countries and patronyms. Whether given names enter the scope is an editorial
decision, not settled here.
