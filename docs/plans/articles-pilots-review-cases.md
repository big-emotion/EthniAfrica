# Articles pilots — semantic review cases (P5)

Written 2026-09-30, **before** the three pilot bodies were drafted, as the test-first step of
P5 in [the plan](articles-refonte-2026-09-30.md). Evidence and open items are recorded in
[the execution evidence file](articles-refonte-2026-09-30-evidence.md).

These are review cases for a human reader, not assertions a unit test can make. The unit test
(`src/lib/articles/__tests__/pilots.test.ts`) only holds the mechanical floor: still draft,
a body exists, every reference resolves, no author-first opener, a real excerpt, an English
deferral. Passing it says nothing about whether the prose is right. Operator review is required.

Evidence base for every case: the approved narration or card text, the publication copy,
`SOURCES.md`, the myth and script reviews in the private workshop, and the source pages listed
as "opened" in the P5 section of the evidence file. Nothing else. A claim that rests only on a
Wikipedia page is dropped or left as a flagged exception.

## Case 1 — `mali-quelle-histoire`

- **Reader's question.** "Mali" is a country; was there really an "empire du Mali", and where
  does the word come from?
- **Primary reader need (personas).** P1 wants a clear answer and a map of what is known; P2
  (readers who hold that the empire was called Manden) must find their objection taken
  seriously, not dismissed.
- **Claims the article may make, and their support.**
  | Claim                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Record source                     |
  | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
  | Federation of Senegal and the Soudanese Republic; independence 20 June 1960; Senegal leaves 20 August; the Assembly makes the Soudanese Republic the Republic of Mali on 22 September 1960                     | s1 (opened), s2 (opened)          |
  | French Soudan's limits differed from present-day Mali on the Mauritania side until the Treaty of Kayes (16 February 1963); the Burkina Faso stretch was fixed by the ICJ in 1986 on the limits at independence | s12 (opened), s13 (opened)        |
  | Niane uses both "empire du Mali" and "empire manden"; Manden lies on the upper Niger between Kangaba and Siguiri                                                                                               | s4 (opened, pp. 141, 143)         |
  | Al-Bakri writes "Malel" in the eleventh century; Niane reads it as the first mention of Mali                                                                                                                   | s4 (opened, p. 150)               |
  | Ibn Battuta reaches the king's seat in 1352; the Catalan Atlas (c. 1375) names a ruler "Musse Melly"                                                                                                           | s5, s6 (opened)                   |
  | Niani as the fourteenth-century capital is contested by archaeology; Fauvelle's 2024 location is a hypothesis                                                                                                  | s5, s7 (opened); s8 (not opened)  |
  | Three meaning explanations (hippopotamus resemblance, "where the king lives", alteration of Manden through the Fulɓe) — none demonstrated                                                                      | s9, s10, s4 p. 153 n. 28 (opened) |
  | Our own earlier statement that inhabitants never used "Mali" was an overstatement                                                                                                                              | approved narration                |
- **Uncertainty that must stay in the sentence.** Why the name was chosen in 1960 is not stated
  by any source consulted; the medieval capital's location is unknown; the word's first
  meaning is not established; the Malel = Mali identification is Niane's.
- **Dropped or flagged.** Senghor's role (Foltz via English Wikipedia only, s3); the 24
  November 1958 date and the December 1958 Bamako congress (Wikipedia only); the party name
  "Union soudanaise-RDA" (not found in the two official pages opened).
- **Improvement over the caption.** The caption lists four usages. The article separates the
  modern naming acts by date, separates name from territory, shows why the capital is not
  located, and gives each meaning explanation its source and its limit.

## Case 2 — `lingala-invente-par-les-belges`

- **Reader's question.** Did the Belgians invent Lingala?
- **Primary reader need.** P2 (speakers with a lived relation to the language, including the
  disagreement already visible in comments) and P3 (a newcomer who met the carousel).
- **Claims and support.**
  | Claim                                                                                                                                                                         | Record source                                                                              |
  | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
  | Bobangi was a river trade language before European occupation; European officers and their troops simplified it from the 1880s                                                | s2/s6 Meeuwis (opened)                                                                     |
  | The pidgin was first called "Bangala" after Bangala Station, named after populations wrongly believed to be called Bangala                                                    | s2/s6 Meeuwis (opened)                                                                     |
  | After the 1893 mutiny the Force publique adopted the language to unify its troops                                                                                             | s5 Castillo (read by the workshop, not opened here)                                        |
  | 1901–1902: Scheut missionaries reform the language and rename it "Lingala" (Meeuwis) — contested: the name already meant "language" in Bobangi before colonisation (Mimpongo) | s6 (opened), s7 (abstract opened)                                                          |
  | Under Mobutu, spread by use, not by law; French stays the only official language                                                                                              | s8 Castillo (workshop reading)                                                             |
  | In 1997 the Kadogos from the East strengthen Swahili in the army                                                                                                              | s9 IPS (workshop reading, no locator)                                                      |
  | 1929: the language was called "bad Bobangi"; in the East some Swahili speakers criticise it                                                                                   | s11 Meeuwis citing Marker (workshop reading; not found in the page fetched here), Castillo |
- **Uncertainty.** Who first said "lingala" is not settled: two named readings, neither crowned.
- **Dropped or flagged.** Artist names tied to the rumba (Wikipédia FR only, s12); 1876 and the
  list of peoples called "Bangala", the Nouvelle-Anvers and Makanza renamings (Mbulamoko and
  Burssens, not read by the workshop); Prunier (via Wikipedia EN, not read); "one of four
  national languages" (caption only, no source). Card 6 and card 8 credit/image mismatch and
  the reused card 2/9 image stay flagged, not resolved.
- **Improvement over the carousel.** It shows that the carousel's "on lui a seulement donné un
  nom écrit" is too narrow on the evidence (Meeuwis describes a reform, not only a spelling),
  separates spoken language, first name and written name, and gives each side of the name
  debate its own source.

## Case 3 — `manden-mande-mandingue-trois-mots`

- **Reader's question.** Manden, Mandé, mandingue: the same thing? Is Mandé a people?
- **Primary reader need.** P2 (readers who call themselves Manden, Mandenka or Mandingue) and
  P1. The correction of our earlier video must be visible, in its approved words.
- **Claims and support.**
  | Claim                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Record source                           |
  | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
  | Correction note, quoted from the approved narration                                                                                                                                                                                   | edition `correctionNote`                |
  | Manden/Mande: the country on the upper Niger between Kangaba and Siguiri; the name later covered all Maninka lands                                                                                                                    | s1 (opened, pp. 141, 153)               |
  | Niane's account of Do and Kiri, the sixteen predecessor kings with variants between schools, Mamadi Kani, the Soso (a Maninka clan of ironworkers), the 1220–1235 war, 1235; this chronology is relative (Delafosse from Ibn Khaldun) | s1 (opened, pp. 143, 149, 151, 153–154) |
  | "L'enfant du lamantin": one transmitted explanation, from the griot Babou Condé as published by Camara Laye (1978)                                                                                                                    | s5 (not opened; in copyright, no URL)   |
  | Koelle's informant names his country "Mánde, or Mandén·ga"; Koelle calls the maps' "Mandingo" an incorrect spelling                                                                                                                   | s2 (opened)                             |
  | Mande as a language family; Manding includes Bamana, Maninka, Jula varieties, Mandinka; Soninke and Kpelle sit in other groups                                                                                                        | s3 (opened, p. 107)                     |
  | Donaldson's fieldwork (2012–2017; Bobo-Dioulasso, Bamako, Banamba) and the N'ko register that links varieties                                                                                                                         | s4 (abstract/summary opened)            |
- **Uncertainty.** No other origin of "Manden" found; one griot's account is not "the tradition of
  the whole country"; Niane's dates are a reconstruction; classification does not decide identity.
- **Dropped or flagged.** Almada 1594, Park 1799, Caillié 1830 and Delafosse 1901 (not
  page-verified by the workshop and not in the record's sources); "Mali attested only from the
  fourteenth century" (conflicts with Niane p. 150 on al-Bakri's Malel — the article defers to
  the Mali article instead of repeating it).
- **Improvement over the video.** It shows the correction and what the earlier video said, keeps
  the three words from being boxed as place/family/people, and dates the sources of each use.
