# Product Brief: EthniAfrica

## Executive Summary

EthniAfrica answers one question: **where do the names of Africa's peoples,
countries, languages and family names come from?** For each name it shows the
name a people gives itself and the names others gave it, side by side and at
the same weight, with every source labelled by tier, oral accounts included.
It is free, open source, French-first and built for the phone.

In its first month the project found an audience faster than it found readers.
The social accounts, about three weeks old at the 30 September audit, reached
roughly 450,000 views in September (Facebook 281.6K, Instagram 85.3K, TikTok
82.5K). The audit counted 8,047 Facebook and 1,737 Instagram followers; the
operator now reports about 10,000 and 2,000. The site, which is what makes the
project more than one more account, received 365 consented visitors, of whom
57 came from Instagram and Facebook. Post-level link clicks were 7 on Facebook
in 28 days and none on Instagram. Search engines already send more than the
networks: Google 105 visitors, chatgpt.com 26.

This brief sets the next phase, October to December 2026: **the reference comes
first.** The work is to connect the audience the networks already bring to the
search engine that holds the answers, and to build a small validating team
before the operator's time shrinks at the end of November.

## The Problem

A reader of the diaspora, or on the continent, wants to know where their
people's name or their own family name comes from. Today they find:

- **Language catalogues** (Glottolog, Ethnologue) that list alternate names
  without saying who named whom, or why. Ethnologue is behind a paywall.
- **Name-meaning sites** with no sources, and **DNA services** that promise an
  ethnic origin their own critics say they cannot deliver.
- **Culture and history media** (Nofi, BY US MEDIA, many social accounts) that
  tell stories well, but offer nothing a reader can search.
- **Primary references** (UNESCO's _General History of Africa_, the British
  Library's Endangered Archives) that are free and serious, but not searchable
  by a people's name and not written for a phone.

Underneath sits a harder problem. What has been written about African names was
mostly written from outside: by Arab and European authors, colonial
administrators and missionary linguists. Local and oral accounts are thinner and
harder to collect. Defaulting to the written outside account is not neutrality.

The project has its own version of the problem. The operator works alone,
alongside a job; a single piece can take a full day. From the end of November
2026 a growing family will leave much less time. A project that depends on one
person's evenings stops when those evenings stop.

## The Solution

- **A name search engine as the product.** A reader types the name they know,
  often an outside name such as « peul », and gets every form the entry is known
  by: where each comes from, what it raises, and what the sources do not settle.
  The page never crowns a form, and it never claims a silence it has not
  declared.
- **A corpus that publishes the claim with its provenance.** Nothing is rejected
  for being weak; everything is labelled `official`, `referenced` or
  `unverified`. Oral knowledge is a source in its own right, identified by its
  carrier, place and consent.
- **Social media as the front door.** Short carousels and reels on a name, a
  people or a myth bring the audience, and each piece should lead to the answer
  on the site.
- **Participation in the shape "readers propose, a small team validates".**
  Readers submit corrections, oral accounts and family names; volunteers check
  and prepare them; the operator decides. This is not open Wikipedia-style
  editing.

## What Makes This Different

- **Built around the name, not the nation-state.** It describes Africa through
  the names its peoples give themselves, rather than through states whose
  borders are recent.
- **Honest where adjacent services overclaim.** Every source carries a tier; a
  contested origin is written as contested; several accounts are shown when
  several exist.
- **Oral and local accounts admitted on the same footing.** This is a working
  answer to a debate Wikimedia's own Oral Citations research has left open.
- **Free and open.** The corpus is under CC BY-SA 4.0, with a public API and
  downloadable data, so researchers and cultural projects can reuse it.
- **A real, concrete project behind the accounts.** It is a reference that stays
  and is consulted, not a feed of posts.

The honest moat is the corpus and its doctrine, not technology: 772 people
records, 805 patronymes and 54 countries already filed, and editorial rules
(tiers, register, contested claims) enforced by automated checks rather than
stated in a document. An account can copy the tone in a week; it cannot copy
that.

## Who This Serves

- **Primary: the francophone African diaspora.** In September, 54% of measured
  site visitors were in France. They are looking for the origin of a people's
  name or a family name, and want an answer they can trust and share.
- **Readers on the continent**, as direct readers, never as informants. Côte
  d'Ivoire is the second country on the site and the first among Facebook
  followers.
- **Holders of a lived account** who can bring an oral or family version of a
  name.
- **Secondary: researchers, teachers and cultural projects** (films, books,
  events) who need a sourced starting point they can cite and reuse.

The personas are needs-based and still unvalidated; no reader has been
interviewed yet (`docs/editorial/audience-personas.md`).

## Success Criteria

The September figures are baselines from consented sessions only, so they are a
floor. Targets are proposals for the operator to confirm.

| Signal                                   | September baseline           | Direction for the phase                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Visitors who run a search                | 106 visitors, 292 searches   | grows month on month                          |
| Searches that reach a result             | 62 visitors clicked a result | share rises                                   |
| Site visits arriving from social         | Instagram 40, Facebook 17    | each post links to its answer; tags reconcile |
| Site visits arriving from search engines | Google 105, chatgpt.com 26   | grows as answer pages are indexed             |
| Returning visitors                       | not measured                 | measured before any app decision              |

| Research volunteers onboarded            | 0                            | at least 1, trained before end of November    |

## Scope (October–December 2026)

**In:**

- The search engine and the corpus, the patronyme entry point included.
- The bridge from social to site: one answer page per published subject, and
  campaign tags that agree between the ledger and the captions.
- Discoverability of answer pages by search engines and AI assistants, which
  already bring more visitors than the networks do.
- One volunteer mission, **content research** (find and check sources, prepare a
  name for review), described in an explicit mission sheet. Recruitment starts
  with a presentation to the Ripat Africa community.
- A measured contribution path, "propose then validate". An oral account is
  accepted only with its carrier's consent; unreviewed proposals stay visible
  as pending, never silently published.
- Social publishing at a cadence one person can hold, below the Q4 roadmap's 4–6
  pieces a week.
- Exploring one grant or partnership; donations are accepted.

**Out, for now:**

- A native mobile app. 83% of site visitors are already on mobile; an
  installable web app (PWA) is the cheaper step, and is reconsidered once
  returning visitors are measured.
- Becoming a media outlet, including long-form YouTube production as a goal.
- Paid advertising and email lead magnets.
- Open editing, and publication in English (the code is already bilingual, so
  this stays a later, cheap step).
- Any partnership with DNA or ancestry-matching services: they overclaim exactly
  where this project refuses to.

Wikidata exchange is not assumed: Wikidata requires CC0, which the corpus's
CC BY-SA licence does not grant as is.

## Vision

In two to three years, success shows in three ways:

- **Product.** Looking up a name on EthniAfrica has become a habit for people
  seeking the origin of their people or their family name, and a sourced
  starting point that researchers and cultural projects cite.
- **Practice.** A small team of contributors proposes and validates, so the
  project no longer depends on one person.
- **Meaning, held with humility.** EthniAfrica is one contributor among others
  to an Africa told through its peoples' names, in which the diaspora and the
  continent start conversations and create things: modern libraries of the past,
  films, a wider cultural influence.

A media arm and conversations with invited speakers can follow. Specialists and
holders of oral knowledge would be quoted for what they say, not borrowed as
authority, which is consistent with the rule that peoples speak first.
