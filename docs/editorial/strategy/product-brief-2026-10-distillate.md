# Product Brief Distillate: EthniAfrica (Oct–Dec 2026)

Companion to [the product brief](product-brief-2026-10.md). Dense on purpose;
each bullet stands alone.

## Operator decisions (2026-10-03, verbatim intent)

- Reference first (search engine + corpus); media is "a different trade", deferred.
  The operator values social media for its immediate feedback and audience, and
  the site as what distinguishes the project from accounts that are only a feed.
- Participation: readers propose, a small team validates. Not open editing.
- Donations, grants and partnerships accepted; running costs manageable, no
  funding urgency.
- Family grows by end of November 2026: publishing must continue at a lower
  pace; new fiches continue but pause first if time runs short.
- A trained volunteer may approve `referenced`-tier sources and check fiches.
  `official` and contested rulings stay with the operator (inferred boundary,
  not stated by the operator; confirm when writing the mission sheet).
- Claude and git are welcome, not prerequisites. The operator can train a
  volunteer on one precise git task. A volunteer who only brings sourced
  information is already valuable.
- Publishing has no schedule: some days ten posts, some nights, none fixed. The
  Monday/Wednesday/Friday plan was not held. Do not impose a cadence; measure
  continuity (≥1 publication per week).
- Targets for end of December accepted: 250 searching visitors/month, ≥65%
  result-click rate, 20 validated proposals, 2 volunteers onboarded.
- Operator will run 5–8 reader interviews in October.
- Audience scope: anyone who recognises themselves in Africa, closely or from
  afar; Caribbean and Haitian diaspora explicitly included.
- Recruitment: Ripat Africa first; universities in Dakar and Abidjan (remote
  from Paris is a stated difficulty).

## Requirements hints (for mission sheets, template, site)

- Volunteer mission 1, publication research: deliver a subject report
  (subject, angle, narrative outline) plus images. Images are
  the slowest step for the operator; rights and licence must travel with each
  image. Operator keeps structure and render.
- Volunteer mission 2, fiche preparation: a fill-in template simpler than the
  strict models in `public/modele-*.json`; every claim carries a tiered source;
  the operator integrates. Template must not require reading the models.
- Each mission sheet states effort (1–2 h/week suggested by the advisor),
  deliverable, weekly report, and limits. Advisor's rule: a volunteer is not an
  entrepreneur who guesses what to do.
- A public "open volunteer roles" section on `/contribute` (advisor idea).
- Oral accounts accepted only with carrier consent (DEC-055 §6, migration 091).
- Unreviewed proposals stay pending, never silently published; an intake cap
  ("pause above N open proposals") was suggested and not decided.
- The Google Forms pilots are owned by a private account: single point of
  failure for intake.
- Social proof idea (advisor): at the moment a reader finds an answer, ask « Que
  pensez-vous de cette information ? Le saviez-vous ? »; answers feed weekly
  testimonials. Must respect consent and the reader-facing register.
- Campaign tags disagree between ledger and captions (Bouët-Willaumez): fix
  before measuring the social-to-site bridge.
- Returning visitors and source clicks are not instrumented today.

## Rejected or deferred, with reason

- Native mobile app: 83% of visitors already on mobile; no measured return
  visits; one more codebase and two stores for a solo operator. PWA first.
  iOS caveat: web push only from iOS 16.4, after Add to Home Screen. Apple's
  February 2024 EU removal of Home Screen web apps was reportedly reversed in
  March 2024: unverified, check before relying on it (French diaspora is in the EU).
- Email lead magnet: advisor's own view, poor fit with open source.
- Paid Meta ads: later option. Advisor's setup, kept for reference: 5–7 €/day,
  five educational pieces, "triangular" launch (lookalike of site visitors,
  interest "Africa", broad).
- Faceless value-proposition format: advisor judged it least relevant.
- Expert podcasts framed as "their authority strengthens ours": conflicts with
  "peoples speak first" and the ban on leading with « Selon X ». Allowed shape:
  invited speakers quoted for what they say. Advisor suggested 5–8 episodes,
  LinkedIn sourcing of historians, Riverside (local 1080p/720p recording),
  Opus Clip for short cuts, and an open-source/volunteer call inside episodes.
  YouTube is the smallest audience (166 subscribers in September).
- DNA or ancestry partnerships: they overclaim (press-reported studies: under
  10% of samples matched a single ethnic group).
- Wikidata exchange: Wikidata is CC0, corpus is CC BY-SA 4.0.

## Content formats from the advisor (not yet in the ledger)

- Demo: someone reacts to a name origin (e.g. Côte d'Ivoire), then shows the
  site screen and the clicks that found it. Possibly AI avatars.
- Education: lists, stories, carousels, face-to-camera; soft call to follow or
  visit the site.
- Testimonial: about one per week, from readers who found an answer.
- Founder storytelling: why the operator created and returned to the project;
  requires a willingness to appear.
- "Find your name in the list" mechanism (viral Caribbean surname post): fits
  the patronyme entry point; legitimate only on result pages the corpus fills,
  otherwise it is a bait hook.
- Shared publishing tool with approval workflow: advisor named « Fidel », Brand
  plan from 22 €/month, 5 spaces, 5 members each. Name unidentified; verify
  before paying.

## Partnership and funding leads (unverified calls and eligibility)

- Wikimedia Rapid Fund: $500–5,000, 1–12 months, needs a Wikimedia-facing
  deliverable and usually a user group partner (Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon groups
  recruit through edit-a-thons and contests).
- Wikimedia Oral Citations research: the open debate the tier policy answers;
  strongest grant narrative.
- British Library EAP (funded by Arcadia): digitisation, fits an oral-recording
  partner.
- OIF digital and cultural programmes; Prince Claus Fund.
- University departments: INALCO (Paris), Cheikh Anta Diop (Dakar), Félix
  Houphouët-Boigny (Abidjan): supervised student work on the research missions.
- Community radios and diaspora associations: oral accounts under consent.
- Comments as demand: "and my people? my name?" requests logged as search
  demand and contribution leads.
- Teachers: a short classroom sheet per name from existing pages.

## Competitive notes

- Nofi, BY US MEDIA: storytelling media, no searchable sourced reference (Nofi's
  2018 figures: 1.8M Facebook, ~500k visits/month; outdated).
- Glottolog (open, CC-BY), Ethnologue (paywall): language-centred name lists,
  no who-named-whom.
- Joshua Project: missionary, admits thin sourcing and duplicates.
- UNESCO GHA and EAP: sources to cite, not competitors. The operator's
  methodological anchor is GHA I (Ki-Zerbo; chapters by Hampâté Bâ, « La
  tradition vivante », and Vansina on oral tradition). A 19-volume UNESCO shelf
  is catalogued in PR #1470.
