## What is fixed, and what can move

- **Next publication:** the essay's intention, presented through concrete names.
- **First sequence, in order:** Mali → Manden/Mandé/mandingue → Dioula → Traoré →
  Keïta/Coulibaly → Macina/Diina. No obligation to finish a subject in one post.
- **Appointments:** Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Dates below are recommended
  planning slots, Europe/Paris, not posts scheduled in an external account.
  No publication hour is selected without audience/time-capacity evidence.
- **Volume:** aim for four to six distinct editorial pieces a week where the
  research supports it. This is an effort envelope, not a quota of new topics.
  The same video on six networks is one piece. A copied caption, reminder or
  identical cross-post does not inflate the count.
- **Composition:** three main appointments plus one to three useful companions:
  a separately written source-led text, an attested-myth carousel, or a sourced
  answer to a reader's question. A complex subject may occupy several main
  appointments; a continuation must answer a different question. Do not split
  one thin answer into three posts or invent a myth to reach six.
- **Readiness:** source review precedes scripting. If a claim is not ready,
  preserve the sequence and shift its successors. Use a documented question,
  correction or source comparison from the current subject, not an unrelated
  ready-made topic. If none is ready, publish fewer pieces and record why.
- **Planning horizon:** the launch on 25 September plus thirteen weeks through
  25 December. The first four weeks are the working sequence; later dates are
  reservations to review, with a major checkpoint on 4 December. December 25
  is a provisional closing slot, not a commitment to work on a holiday.

The four historical pillars remain useful angle labels, not a mechanical
rotation that interrupts a coherent sequence. An audience spike can deepen a
chapter; it cannot indefinitely displace the next geographic opening.

## The narrative route

**A place name → ways of naming languages and communities → transmission of a
family name → obligations between people → institutions of government.**

The first chapter stays in the Mali–Côte d'Ivoire historical space. It then
follows questions of trade, naming and political authority into Côte d'Ivoire
and Ghana, and opens a new chapter in the Congo basin. Comparison with Igbo
institutions follows because the question of governing has been established.
These are editorial connections, not claims of migration, common descent or
historical influence between every consecutive society.

France is a reading position throughout: explain what a diaspora reader can
recognise, ask relatives about, or research. Do not infer an individual person's
origin, clan, rights or ancestry from their surname.

## Audience review and bounded adaptation

Every Friday, read the last eligible pieces (at least 72 hours old); compare
seven-day and then 28-day results within the same network and format. Record
source date, age, duration, impressions/views as defined by that platform,
retention if exposed, saves, shares, meaningful questions, profile/link activity
and matching site arrivals where attributable. No invented targets from missing
retention data; no cross-platform sum of views.

Use the evidence to make one decision: deepen, clarify, continue, or move the
chapter. Prioritise a factual correction as soon as it is verified, regardless
of age. Give a complex question one extra main slot before rebalancing the
calendar; a continuing research need can take longer, but its effect on the next
regional chapter must be explicit. Preserve a reasoned opening to another region
rather than indefinitely extending the highest-view community.

Keep two weeks of candidate briefs, not twelve weeks of locked scripts. At the
4 December checkpoint assess production hours, unfinished source work, unfiled
URLs, recurring misunderstandings and who is engaging. Geography comes from
available native audience aggregates and voluntary comments, never surname-based
inference. Low-volume country samples are directional only.

## Continental horizon and English

This quarter builds depth in West and Central Africa and opens the question of
political diversity in Nigeria. It is not a claim to have covered the continent.
Subsequent research chapters should deliberately include:

1. **North Africa and Sahara–Sahel:** names, language and changing belonging;
   identify the next precise name after the December review. Prior Amazigh
   content exists, but its performance was not collected this run.
2. **East Africa and the Indian Ocean:** how coastal names and language use
   travel. Swahili is already published: Sep 21 **192 YouTube / 49 TikTok views**
   are too young to judge and do not justify repeating its etymology.
3. **Southern Africa and the Great Lakes:** names of places and institutions,
   investigated case by case; no comparable for a precise next angle collected.
4. **African island societies and diasporas:** mobility, language and names,
   not an assumption that migration preserves an unchanged culture; exact-angle
   performance unmeasured.

These are research horizons, not publication-ready country lists. Each needs a
named question, primary/academic sources, a corpus home, a distinct angle and an
updated comparable before taking a slot.

An English editorial line is later work with its own reader questions, subject
selection and production budget. Its audience may be in Africa as well as in
diasporas. Before launching, validate capacity against the French workload,
prepare at least two researched English briefs and a separate measurement plan,
and resolve how original English pieces relate to the existing bilingual content
rules. Do not change `SITE_LOCALE_MODE` or assume that translation readiness alone
authorises an English launch. The December review may scope a pilot; no English
publication or extra weekly workload is committed in this quarter.
