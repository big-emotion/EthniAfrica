# Social series — the fixed frame

Decided with the operator on 2026-10-13, from the audience audit of the same day
(`docs/audience/audit-2026-10-13.md`). This is the structure every social
publication slot is filled from. It fixes **which series exist, when they run,
and how every episode of a series is built**; it does not list future subjects.
Episodes are chosen run by run by `/ethniafrica-content-strategist`, which states
the comparable numbers for each proposal, and produced through
`/ethniafrica-social-production`.

Why a fixed frame: the operator asked for something regular and organised, and
the measurements support fewer, better pieces. Meta counted 78 pieces published
in the week to 10-10 while Facebook's daily views fell under 4K and TikTok lost
31.6% in seven days. Volume is not proven to be the cause; it is the one lever
not yet tried. Reader-facing lines below are French, as published.

## How an episode is built

One script per episode, in fixed beats, so the carousel and the video come from
the same text and the video costs the cards plus the voice.

| Series | Beats                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1      | The name and the question · what it may mean · who gave it, and when · the earlier name or the inhabitants' name · the other readings · the closing line                                                                     |
| 2      | The name everyone knows · who gave it (neighbour, trader, coloniser, linguist) · the name they give themselves · why the gap · many have adopted the given name today; that was not always so · the question to readers      |
| 3      | The name · where it is carried · what it tells · its forms from country to country · « Et ton nom, d'où vient-il ? »                                                                                                         |
| 4      | The word as it is heard · its language and first meaning · how it reached Africa and who used it · what it was used to say · the word people use for themselves                                                              |
| 5      | The people on the map · its name on each side of the border · who drew the line, and when · what the line changed · the closing line                                                                                         |
| 6      | Africa (who named the continent) · Soudan, « le pays des Noirs » · Touareg, Yoruba, Zoulou: three language families · « Afrique noire », « subsaharienne » · Éthiopie and Guinée as names for all of Africa · the conclusion |
| 7      | As series 1                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

**"The other readings" replaces "what we do not know yet"** (operator,
2026-10-13). Episodes are researched as far as the sources go and prepared ahead
of their slot; the episode presents the reading it retains and says plainly that
others exist, attributed, never ranked by origin (`docs/productions/README.md`,
"Never an assertion"). A claim resting only on a machine-written synthesis is
researched before it is scripted (for example `PAT_KOFI`, `PAT_KWAME` and
`PAT_KONE` on 2026-10-13).

## Opening, closing and length of every video

Decided by the operator on 2026-10-19, on the Burkina Faso episode (series 1,
episode 19). The fixed spoken opening, closing and long-first method apply to
every video script from that date. Visual and network-cut choices may have
later episode decisions.

- **Opening, spoken first:** « Aujourd'hui, nous allons parler de l'origine du
  nom [nom]. » followed at once by the recurring introduction « Avec
  EthniAfrica, on part des noms qu'on emploie en français pour mieux comprendre
  l'Afrique et ses peuples. » The next sentence states the episode's concrete
  fact or question and leads straight into the first explanation. Keep the
  introduction to that one sentence; the series title may appear on screen.
  In the Burkina Faso video the opening sentence was said over the opener
  card and the introduction over the intro bumper, its own beat right after
  it; the closing was said over the end card (`social/series-idents/`,
  operator's decision of 19 October 2026).
- **Why the introduction says « en français »:** the starting point is the
  name used in French. It does not mean the name comes from French, always
  differs from local names, or is colonial. Each case is explained from its
  sources, pejorative, racialising or political uses included.
- **Closing, spoken last** (operator, 2026-10-27: « Tu changes la formule pour
  toutes les vidéos, oui »), in this order: « Vous pouvez continuer la
  recherche sur EthniAfrica et vous abonner pour découvrir de nouvelles
  publications. EthniAfrica : l'importance de connaître nos noms. » From
  2026-10-19 to 2026-10-26 it was « L'importance de connaître nos noms. Vous
  pouvez continuer la recherche sur EthniAfrica, lien en profil. »; pieces
  published then keep it. It replaces, at the end of the video, both the
  signature line and the search sentence « Cherchez [nom] sur EthniAfrica,
  lien en profil. ». The signed « nous pensons » paragraph still comes just
  before it. The same closing ends every carousel and every caption, and
  « L'importance de connaître nos noms. » replaces the signature line in every
  account's bio. How the closing cards and the video ending print it is a
  design-system rule (Cartes 1.10.0, accepted on 2026-10-19, words of
  2026-10-27): the invitation first, on screen as in the voice, then the line,
  the block in its 1.10.0 place. The carousel renderer and
  the series video engine draw it since PR #1894: the closing card's chunks
  are the five printed lines, the invitation's three first, and a closing card
  no longer prints a sentence of its own. The
  satellite map videos and the clipped reels end the same way since the
  operator's decision of 2026-10-19: they print the same closing line and
  invitation, each in its own ending's sizes and place.
- **Long first, refined after:** the script tells the whole story the sources
  support, as a long video with chapters, then is reduced with the operator
  round by round. For a country name in series 1 the thread runs: the words of
  the name · the peoples behind each word and how they came to the region ·
  what existed before the colony · how the colony drew the frame · how the
  present name was chosen · a short summary of the name's origin · the reframe
  · the « nous pensons » paragraph · the closing. Unity, the operator's
  conviction, belongs to the « nous pensons » paragraph and stays distinct
  from the historical findings.
- **Length follows the story.** For Burkina Faso, the operator chose the same
  4 min 15 cut on TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube. A video over 3 minutes is a regular video on
  YouTube, not a Short; Instagram does not recommend reels over 3 minutes to new
  audiences; TikTok's upload length is checked in the app before posting.
- **For Burkina Faso, the animated satellite map carries the video**
  (operator, 2026-10-19; Cartes 1.11.0): whenever the narration situates something, the screen is a
  map, opened by the satellite globe; it must be understood without sound.
  Scripts are written in map phases and picture phases; a picture is a phase
  of whole sentences (6 s at least), never a short insert between two maps, and
  carries a fact the map cannot show.

### Episode 20: Togo format decision

The operator revised the Togo episode for TikTok, Instagram and Facebook in
October 2026. The fixed spoken opening, introduction, signed paragraph and
closing still apply. The opening sentence plays over checked footage of
Togoville rather than a globe or opener card. Claude Design must approve that
visual change before production.

The three networks have separate scripts, chapter sequences and estimated
durations:

| Network   | Estimated duration   | Chapter progression                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| TikTok    | 1 min 40 to 1 min 50 | Locate Togo, Lake Togo and Togoville; compare two attributed meanings; trace the name from the 1884 agreement through the colony to the independent country; show that other peoples have their own histories.                        |
| Instagram | 2 min 35 to 2 min 45 | Locate the village; compare three local explanations; introduce families living there before the colony; trace the colonial use; explain the British and French outcomes; show that the country's history extends beyond the village. |
| Facebook  | 3 min 35 to 3 min 50 | Locate the village; give more room to competing readings; introduce the families and other peoples before colonisation; trace the German colony, the two later administrations and independence; return to Togoville today.           |

Each cut then has its separate signed « nous pensons » paragraph and the fixed closing.
These are editorial estimates and episode chapters, not platform limits or a
template for later episodes. Keep the long sourced narrative as their base.

For these cuts, use a precise, verified present-day map only to locate the
country, lake, village or another place the narration needs to situate. Show
dated documents, source cards, footage and motion text for the naming accounts
and political steps. Do not depict an approximate historical border or route
as exact. Every visual phase must carry its meaning without sound.

## What changed on 19 October 2026

The Burkina Faso episode (series 1, episode 19) replaced a 87-second card video
with a long episode and changed the production process. The full path is the
skill's [long-episode recipe](../../../.claude/skills/ethniafrica-social-production/references/long-episode-recipe.md).

**New moments in the process**, each before the step it protects:

1. **An animatic artifact** of the whole cut, shot by shot, with what the
   screen shows without sound, before any render.
2. **A preview at one frame per second** with the real voice, in a few minutes.
3. **A pre-flight of every package check** before the final render: design
   label, no demo marker, idents, research dossier and destination, image
   rights. The episode took four full renders for want of it.
4. **The composed thumbnail**, delivered with the package.
5. **One visible handover folder** for the operator after approval 3 (video,
   thumbnail, one document with every text to paste), moved to the published
   pieces once posted; then the URLs are recorded in the checkpoint and the
   production ledger.

**Rules changed:** the opening and closing above; long first and length
following the story; the map-led video; captions with targeted hashtags; the
thumbnail; the fiches corrected and released before posting so the profile link
answers what the video says.

## Outside the series

Two kinds of post sit outside the frame and never take a series slot unless
the operator moves one.

- **Exceptional posts**: an independence day, an international or special day,
  a historical figure. Free format, carousel or video, and free structure. The
  strategist lists the dated opportunities of the next 30 days at each run;
  independence dates come from the source recorded in `colonial-periods.md`.
  A name-origin exceptional post is filed in the ledger like any name piece;
  any other one stays in the private library.
- **Reposts of other accounts' content**: allowed, with the rights cleared by
  the operator for each piece. A repost is not a produced piece, does not
  count in the four-to-six envelope, and is filed in the private library
  unless its caption frames it as a name or word origin.

## Formats per episode

- **A video every episode.** Reels are the better-observed way to reach new
  people on Instagram and Facebook (98.6% of Instagram views were reels, audit
  of 2026-10-13); Shorts on YouTube.
- **A carousel only when the episode takes apart an attested received idea**,
  the rule the name-series ledger already applies. On TikTok carousels earn
  reach of their own (« Lingala » 39K, « Jula » 14K, « Yamoussoukro » 963 as of
  2026-10-13), so a carousel, when there is one, goes there first.
- Visual: the accepted card system in `social/design-system/`, plus one band
  carrying the series name and the episode number. No separate design per
  series.

## Leading readers to the site

Network reach is not reaching the site: 448K Instagram views in 30 days, 5
consented visitors from all networks in the last week, 0 link clicks on the
biggest posts. The one link that converts is the profile link (105 taps in 30
days).

- **Every caption ends with the same sentence, no link block, no hashtag wall**
  (since 2026-10-27): « Vous pouvez continuer la recherche sur EthniAfrica et
  vous abonner pour découvrir de nouvelles publications. EthniAfrica :
  l'importance de connaître nos noms. » From 2026-10-19 to 2026-10-26 it was
  « L'importance de connaître nos noms. Vous pouvez continuer la recherche sur
  EthniAfrica, lien en profil. », which replaced « Cherchez [nom] sur
  EthniAfrica, lien en profil. ».
- **A short line of targeted hashtags** (operator, 2026-10-19), on every
  network, just before that closing sentence so the caption still ends with
  it: four or five tags about the episode, always ending with `#EthniAfrica`
  (Burkina Faso: `#BurkinaFaso #Afrique #HistoireAfricaine #Toponymie
#EthniAfrica`). A few precise tags help a platform file the video for the
  people who follow those subjects; a wall of them reads as advertising.
- **Every video ships one composed thumbnail** (operator, 2026-10-19), the same
  image for every network, 1080 × 1920 with its essentials inside the profile
  grid's 3:4 crop: the series band, the episode's title, the subject drawn in
  the series' world (for a country, its outline in its flag's colours on the
  series' satellite imagery), the imagery credit and the EthniAfrica
  signature. No personality photographs. The operator publishes it.
- **Retired on 2026-10-19:** every reel and every carousel ended with the
  project's signature, word for word, and every account's bio carried it
  (decided 2026-10-15). The closing in « Opening, closing and length of every
  video » replaces it on videos, carousels, captions and bios:
  « Connaître nos noms, décoloniser leur emploi, nous nommer nous-mêmes. »
  It comes after the series' fixed closing line, never in its place, and
  repeats nothing of it. Its meaning, its limits and the four kinds of use
  behind « décoloniser leur emploi » are in
  [the essay, section 5.6](../essais/afrique-nest-pas-un-tout-2026-10-13.md).
  How it sits on the cards is a design-system rule, not decided here.
- **The profile link stays fixed** on the search page; the episode-specific link
  goes in the **Instagram story link sticker**, the only place where a link is
  one tap away and changes with every episode:
  `https://ethniafrica.com/fr/atlas/recherche/<nom>?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=story&utm_campaign=<series>-<episode>`.
  Since 2026-10-18 the episode link goes to the name's own address (REQ-200),
  no longer to the search page with `?q=<nom>`; `<nom>` is the name as the
  corpus spells it, in lower case, accents kept, and the address must be in
  the sitemap or answer 200
  ([name addresses](../../../.claude/skills/ethniafrica-social-production/references/publication-package.md#name-addresses)).
- **Every post is filed and carries a campaign code** `<series>-<episode>`
  (`pays-14`, `nom-recu-03`, `nom-famille-06`, `mots-02`, `frontieres-01`,
  `afrique-tout-01`, `villes-01`), so each series can be credited with the
  readers it sends.
- **Landing pages are fixed before a series sends readers to them** (handoff to
  `/ethniafrica-experience-optimizer`): the result-page timeline emits no click
  event; `/fr/atlas/pays/CIV`, `FLG_MANDE`, the `PAT_*` template and `PPL_ZARMA`
  are dead ends or bounce-throughs. Series 7 waits for place fiches: the site
  has 3 place pages in its sitemap and none was visited.

## Editorial positions recorded with this frame

- **Afrique**: the episode presents one hypothesis and says explicitly that
  there are several (an Afri people near Carthage, Phoenician, Latin and Greek
  readings).
- **Borders**: the Berlin conference set the rules of the partition; the lines
  were drawn by treaties between European powers, mostly 1885–1914; in 1964
  African states chose to keep them.
- **Unity** (series 6) is a signed position, written « nous pensons que… »,
  never presented as a sourced fact. Any figure on Africa's resources enriching
  other countries carries its source.
- **Every episode carries part of the signed position** (operator, 2026-10-15:
  « Chaque vidéo doit être vue comme une possibilité de partager la morale »).
  A viewer may see one episode only, so the « nous pensons » paragraph is no
  longer kept for episode 6. Episode 6 still develops it in full, with the
  series' closing line. The paragraph closes each video, set apart from the
  sourced facts, and never blames the Africans who use a word.
- **Third-party clips**: the operator clears the rights for each clip before
  publication.
