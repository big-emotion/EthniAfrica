# AFRIK corpus consistency report, November 2026

A read-only survey of how the fiches in `dataset/source/afrik` agree with each
other. It lists every place where two linked fiches say different things, where
a link has no return, and where one work or one name is written in several ways.
No fiche was edited to produce it, and it proposes no correction: each line is a
question for the editor who owns the fiche.

Read with [the doctrine](doctrine.md). Two of its rules shape this report. No
name form is promoted over another, so the name sections list every variant side
by side and never say which one is right. Every origin is a hypothesis, so a
date or a figure that differs between two fiches can be two sources that
disagree, not a mistake; the report shows the disagreement and leaves the
reading to the fiche.

## What was found

No fiche points at an id that does not exist or has been retired. The main
inconsistencies are elsewhere: the same work cited under several titles (634
addresses), people and languages labelled differently from one fiche to the
next (68 peoples, 31 languages), some of them by a row whose label names a
different group, nine language families that disagree outright, 51 population
figures that differ by two times or more between a people fiche and a country
fiche, and 26 surname alliances recorded on one side only. The longest lists
(country membership, family sample lists, surnames a people fiche never writes,
language codes with no language fiche) measure how far the cross links reach
more than they prove errors.

The longest sections are cut at their first 25 entries here; every entry,
with its fiche path and JSON path, is in
[the full lists](corpus-consistency-2026-11-full-lists.md).

## Scope and date

Measured on 2026-11-03 against `origin/recette` at commit `e23a0a64`, over
1,586 fiches: peoples, countries, languages, language families, surnames,
relations, migrations, places, words and dossiers. Other sessions were editing
fiches while this ran, so a line may already be fixed, or a new one may have
appeared, by the time it is read. Rerun before acting on a line.

Left out: the curator worksheets whose names start with `_` (they declare no
link the site relies on), `archive/`, and `logs/`. The two retirement ledgers,
`_retired-identifiers.json` and `patronymes/_retired-identifiers.json`, were read
as the list of retired ids, not as fiches.

`npx tsx scripts/validateAfrikData.ts` passed all 54 of its checks on the same
commit, with no error. This report goes further than the validator in the
directions the request named, and most of what it lists is outside what the
validator enforces.

## Method

Everything was measured by a script that reads the JSON and writes nothing in
the repository. For each kind:

1. **Ids.** Every string in every fiche was searched for an id (`PPL_`, `FLG_`,
   `PAT_`, `REL_`, `MGR_`, `DOS_`, `LOC_`, `WRD_`): 3,489 occurrences, 3,488 in
   id fields and one in prose. Each was looked up among the fiches and in both
   retirement ledgers. Country codes were looked up among the country fiches.
   Language codes were looked up among the language fiches, in
   `famille_linguistique/langue_par_famille.csv`, and in the ISO 639-3 code
   table. The 4,545 `sourceRefs` were looked up among the `sourceKey` values of
   their own fiche.
2. **Names.** For each people, every label another fiche writes beside its id
   (country fiches, family fiches, language fiches) was set against the people
   fiche's own names: `nameMain`, the self-name, the exonyms and the names in
   `nameHistory`. Two labels count as variants when their first form differs
   after ignoring case, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and a final plural _s_.
   Accents are kept: _Sérère_ and _Serer_ are listed. A gloss in brackets is
   not a variant. The same was done for languages, by ISO code, and for
   surnames, by the spellings each surname fiche lists.
3. **Contradictions.** Family: the people fiche's `languageFamilyId` against
   the family a country fiche, a family fiche or a language fiche names for it.
   Countries: the people fiche's two country lists against each other and
   against the country fiches. Population: the people fiche's figure for a
   country against the country fiche's figure for the same people, reported
   when one is at least twice the other. Dates: kingdoms of the same name on
   several country fiches.
4. **Sources.** All 18,095 source entries were grouped by address (scheme,
   `www.` and a final slash ignored) and by title (case, accents and spacing
   ignored).
5. **Return links.** Surname alliances against the partner's alliances;
   relation fiches against the two people fiches; migrations, places and
   surnames against the people fiches they name; language and family fiches
   against the people fiches filed under them.

### How the order is set

The request asked for the findings sorted by how many readers they affect. The
only reader measurement in the repository is
[the audience audit of 2026-10-13](../audience/audit-2026-10-13.md), which
gives visitors for ten fiche pages (`FLG_MANDE` and `PPL_FULA` 10 each; `CIV`,
`PPL_BETE`, `PPL_ZARMA` 7; `PAT_TRAORE`, `KEN`, `PPL_MUCUVAL`, `COG` 6;
`PAT_DIALLO` 5). Every fiche is one page, so each finding is given the set of
pages that show it. Kinds, sections and rows are sorted first by the measured
visitors of those pages, then by how many pages they are. With so small a
sample, the second number decides most of the order; read it as reach, not as
traffic.

In the tables, `″` repeats the cell above, and each place is written as the
fiche path, relative to `dataset/source/afrik/`, followed by its JSON path.

## 1. One work cited under different titles or addresses

The largest group by reach. Most rows are one work cited under titles that differ only in punctuation, capitals, a subtitle or an added author and year; those are harmless to read but split one work into several entries for whoever merges sources later. A few deserve a closer look because the titles name different things at one address: the Roscoe scan of _The Baganda_ is also cited under the names of single clan stories it contains, and broad addresses such as `ethnologue.com`, `cia.gov/the-world-factbook` or the Wiktionary list of codes stand for many different pages, so the title is the only thing that says which one was read.

Five titles point at two addresses each, mostly two scans of the same book on archive.org.
