# EthniAfrica doctrine

The one statement of what EthniAfrica is for. Every page, fiche, publication
and feature answers to it. Approved by the operator on 2026-10-08, from the
funding presentation of the same day
(`strategy/presentation-2026-10/`), which is one rendering of this file.

It replaces the purpose doctrine, the October product brief and its
distillate, and the name-history vision note; they are in git history. Four
documents saying nearly the same thing drift apart, and an agent reading one
of them re-derives the rest differently.

## 1. The rule

> A reader searches for a name. The result page explains **the origin of the
> name**, then **its different names**, and for each one **its history**:
> **qui a nommé qui, quand, pourquoi** — who named whom, when, and why —
> going back as far as the sources allow.

Every name is a story, and the story is the encounter between peoples that
produced it. The name a people gives itself (endonym) and the names others
gave it (exonyms) stand side by side; none is crowned.

People, culture, religion, politics and economics come into the story, and
they will: tracing a name crosses all of them. They come in to tell the
history of the name, never instead of it.

What the result page shows, in this order:

1. the name searched, and where it comes from;
2. every name the same subject goes by;
3. for each of those names, its history: who gave it, to whom, when, why,
   back as far as possible;
4. the sources of each claim, with their tier.

## 2. Scope

**Now, five types of names:**

| Type            | Example          |
| --------------- | ---------------- |
| People          | Peul, Fulɓe, Pël |
| Language        | Lingala          |
| Country         | Côte d'Ivoire    |
| Language family | Bantou           |
| Family name     | Traoré           |

**Plus one free type**, for a word whose history explains Africa through
its names without fitting the five: glossary terms such as « racisme »,
« nation », « État », « esclavage ».

**Next:** places (Goma), customs, the names of things and
words, including words that went into other languages. Then the diasporas and
the Creoles.

This is onomastics, combined with etymology for the names of things and words.

## 3. The problem

There is no unified digital source to search Africa's history through its
names. People make do with Wikipedia and search engines, language catalogues,
name-meaning sites with no sources, DNA tests or the media. Nothing brings it
together, and nothing shows the links between peoples.

Above all, Africa is read inside its national borders: borders mostly drawn
less than 140 years ago, independences barely more than 60 years old. The
history before them is rarely taught; people have to find it for themselves.

Adjacent services, DNA tests included, are named as what people use today.
They are never criticised.

## 4. Our answer

- **A search engine for names.** Every piece of information is shown with its
  source, graded `official`, `referenced` or `unverified`.
- **Oral tradition on an equal footing**, identified by its carrier and place,
  published with their consent.
- **Social media as the front door.** Short formats and video, because people
  read less and watch more. Each piece leads to the answer on the site.
- **Participation.** Readers propose, a small team validates.

## 5. What sets us apart

- **One angle: the history of the name.** Names reveal what is hidden:
  peoples that grew from the same branch, or from occupational groups.
- **Transmitters, not researchers.** We carry on the work of historians and
  linguists and add oral tradition to it, following the method of UNESCO's
  _General History of Africa_ (vol. I, _Methodology and African Prehistory_,
  ed. Joseph Ki-Zerbo).
- **Free and reusable.** The site and the data under CC BY-SA 4.0, with a
  public API.

## 6. Who it serves

- The French-speaking African diaspora, then English- and Spanish-speaking.
- The Caribbean and Haitian diasporas, whose names and memories of origin
  lead back to African peoples.
- The continent, as readers.
- Researchers, teachers and cultural projects who need a sourced starting
  point they can cite and reuse.

## 7. The editorial rule

Every publication, on the site or on social media, tells the history of a
name of one of the five types or the free type, and answers « qui a nommé
qui, quand, pourquoi ». It links to that name's answer page.

## 8. Who carries it

An independent project incubated by the Big Emotion agency, carried by one
person with a few part-time volunteers. The team it aims for: a paid
developer, an expense allowance for social media moderation, and trained
volunteers for source and data checks and research for publications
(`volunteers/`).
