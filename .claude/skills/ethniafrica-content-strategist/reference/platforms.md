# Platform doctrine — what goes where, how often, for whom

Five channels, five different jobs. The mistake this file exists to prevent is
treating them as one audience receiving one video in five aspect ratios.

**The operator is a decision-maker, not a social media specialist.** Every
recommendation drawn from this file must arrive as a directive — do this, on
this channel, this often, because of this number — not as a menu of options.

## The two jobs, and why no single channel does both

Measured 2026-09-07, and reconfirmed with a reversal on 2026-09-14:

- **Reach lives on YouTube Shorts.** 16 Shorts, ~9,800 views in 28 days, 63
  subscribers. Still roughly the largest reach of any channel here, and it
  compounds: a Short keeps earning views for weeks.
- **Traffic no longer lives on LinkedIn — it moved to Instagram's bio link.**
  On 09-07, LinkedIn sent 45 % of all site visitors. On 09-13–09-14, LinkedIn
  sent **zero** while Instagram's bio link sent **28** (bouncing 4 %, against
  LinkedIn's own historical bounce of 35–59 %). LinkedIn's studio was not read
  this cycle, so this is not yet "LinkedIn is dead" — see its own section
  below — but the traffic-engine role it held in the first report has not held.

Neither Shorts-style channel does the traffic job on its own: YouTube Shorts
has **no clickable link in the feed** — a viewer must open the profile and find
the bio link, which almost none do. Instagram Reels has the same feed
limitation, but its **profile bio link converts**, measured for the first time
this cycle, and its **story link sticker** (see below) is the fastest path to a
click the whole plan has.

So: **do not judge a YouTube short by the traffic it sends, and do not judge a
LinkedIn or Instagram post's reach by its view count** — judge Instagram by
what the bio link and story stickers send, LinkedIn by clicks once its studio
is read again. Judging a reach channel by a traffic metric, or a traffic
channel by a reach metric, is how a working channel gets killed.

## Per channel

### YouTube Shorts — the reach engine

- **Audience**: broad, algorithmic, francophone; discovery is feed-driven, and a
  short keeps earning views for weeks, unlike every other platform here.
- **Job**: notoriety and subscribers. This is where the corpus meets people who
  have never heard of it.
- **Frequency**: 3 per week, fixed days. Consistency matters more than volume;
  the algorithm rewards a predictable cadence and punishes gaps.
- **What works, measured 09-14**: 16 Shorts, ~9,790 views in 28 days, 63
  subscribers. Retention separates two batches cleanly: the 09-05→09-09 Shorts
  hold 63–97 % of their length; the 09-12 batch holds only 53–67 %. Views
  range from 1,508 (Ghana, best) down to 115 (Krou); the channel-presentation
  video from the earlier baseline landed 13 — institutional self-description
  still does not travel here, only the subject does.
- **Zero comments across all 16 Shorts.** Views without a single comment,
  ~9.8 K of them — reach without any measured conversation. Watch time is the
  only signal available; there is nothing to read for what viewers understood
  or disputed, unlike TikTok's comments.
- **The link problem**: put the site link in the bio, and name it out loud at
  the end of the video. A pinned comment carrying the link is the second-best
  lever. Never assume a viewer will hunt for it — Plausible has attributed
  exactly 1 visitor to YouTube since collection began.

### LinkedIn — traffic engine, now stalled on video

- **Audience**: professional and institutional — researchers, NGOs, teachers,
  the professional diaspora. The most likely to cite the atlas, link to it, and
  bring an institutional contact.
- **Job**: qualified traffic and credibility. Not reach.
- **Frequency**: 1 to 2 per week, Tuesday to Thursday, morning — **the plan's
  Phase-3 cadence, opened early on 09-11**, and it is not doing the job Phase 3
  was meant for.
- **What works, measured**: still nothing video. On 09-07 the three video
  posts produced zero clicks (24, 60, 78 impressions); reread directly from
  the page's per-post analytics on 09-14, the five 09-12 LinkedIn video posts
  show **0–2 impressions and 0 clicks each** — worse than 09-07, not better,
  despite the account growing to 34 followers. Video does not work on this
  channel at any scale measured so far.
- **The company page's own studio now shows the collapse too**: 15-day content
  stats (29/08–12/09) total 412 impressions, 0 reactions, 0 comments across
  the whole page. This is not one bad post — it is every post in the window.
- **Stop cross-posting the video cut here.** It has never once produced a
  click across two measurement windows five weeks apart. The pillar
  publications should still get a LinkedIn presence, but as **text with a
  link** — the only format proven to convert on this channel (2 clicks each
  at 6.45 % and 4 % CTR, 09-07) — never as the video re-upload.
- **Publish from the personal profile, not only the company page.** Whatever
  LinkedIn traffic existed on 09-07 (45 % of visitors) came mostly through the
  personal profile, not the four clicks the company page's own posts produced.
  The company page's 09-14 numbers (412 impressions, 0 engagement) confirm the
  page alone does not carry the channel.
- **Unmeasured, not dead — read the personal profile's post analytics next
  session** before deciding whether LinkedIn's traffic role is gone or just
  moved off the company page entirely.

### Facebook — the diaspora and community engine

- **Audience**: the oldest of the five (35–65), and the strongest francophone
  African-diaspora community presence. Groups are where this subject actually
  circulates.
- **Job**: traffic and community. **The only channel where a link inside the
  post is normal, clickable and unpunished.**
- **Frequency**: 3 per week, mirroring the Shorts, plus deliberate sharing into
  relevant groups — the group share, not the page post, is the lever.
- **State, 28 days to 09-12**: 9.2 K views, 98.8 % from non-followers, 113
  interactions, 50 follows, 59 page visits, **0 conversations** — up from 6
  followers at the 09-07 baseline, but still no group-share activity measured.
  A page with no community is a broadcast into an empty room; the group
  strategy is what makes this channel exist at all, and it has not started.

### Instagram Reels — the brand engine, and now the only proven traffic

- **Audience**: 25–40, visual-first. Saves and shares are the signals that
  matter; likes are noise.
- **Job**: brand and aesthetic credibility — **plus traffic, as of 09-13**, the
  first measured evidence any channel here reliably converts to a site visit.
- **Frequency**: 3 per week, same cuts as the Shorts, plus a story on every
  publication carrying two stickers: the link sticker — stories are the only
  place a link is one tap away — and the « Questions » sticker, the cheapest
  reply the platform offers, carrying the question pinned under the TikTok post.
- **State, 09-14**: 725 followers (up from 2 at the 09-07 baseline), 12,158
  views in 30 days, 93.9 % from non-followers, 1,482 interactions, 193 profile
  visits, 16 external link taps.
- **The bio link converts.** 28 Plausible visitors arrived via
  `l.instagram.com` on 09-13 alone — 4 % bounce, all mobile, 22 of 28 in
  France — attributable to the profile, not to any tagged post link. 16
  measured link taps against 28 measured visits: the two counters likely
  measure different things (a profile-link tap versus any Instagram-originated
  visit, story links included); unverified, treat both as real rather than
  reconciled.
- **Where those visitors went says what to build next**: six patronyme fiches
  matching the Mali name carousels, and the three peuple fiches those
  carousels named. The profile's bio link is doing real work; what it should
  point to (the profile itself, or a landing built for whichever pillar just
  shipped) is the open decision — see the plan below.

### TikTok — the youngest reach, now proven for argument

- **Audience**: the youngest (16–30), highest velocity, lowest loyalty. A video
  either travels in 48 hours or never.
- **Job**: reach among a younger francophone audience the other channels do not
  touch.
- **Frequency**: daily is ideal, 3 per week is the floor.
- **Caption**: « tu », short sentences, the source line kept, one line asking
  for a comment rather than a tag, and a pinned comment carrying a question
  anyone can answer from their own life. Operator decision of 2026-09-13,
  **unmeasured**: it replaces the long description of 2026-09-12, which rested
  on TikTok's own claim of three times the views. The comment is the signal
  missing everywhere — one in a month across four networks (message audit,
  2026-09-13) — and the phase 1 review of 3–4 October keeps whichever form wins.
- **State, 09-14**: 9.1 K video views in 7 days, 123 profile views, 396 likes,
  9 comments, 18 shares — up from 0 followers / 0 likes at the 09-07 baseline;
  the videos are confirmed live. Traffic is 94.8 % For You, so reach is
  algorithmic, not follower-driven. Search brought some viewers in on their
  own terms: the top queries are variants of the true-size-of-Africa map
  vote, three of five.
- **This is the only channel with real public argument in the comments.** The
  Dioula carousel is contested on its central claim by at least two
  commenters offering competing etymologies — read it before reusing the
  claim (`/afrik-curator`). An Amazigh-carousel comment misreads the post as
  claiming one group owns Algeria — the misreading is itself a signal that
  the framing needs a clearer disclaimer next time this subject repeats.
- **Known constraint**: automated video upload does not work on this platform in
  this environment. The file registers and the composer never advances. TikTok
  publishing is manual, always.

## The frequency question, answered once

The launch plan's cadence — **3 videos per week, Monday / Wednesday / Friday,
batched on Sunday** — is sound and is already running. Do not propose a
different cadence without a measured reason.

What changes per channel is not the cadence but the **cut and the caption**:
one production, publications on the same days across Shorts, Reels, TikTok and
Facebook, plus the Instagram story with its link and Questions stickers on
every publication, and **one separate weekly LinkedIn post that is not the
video** — because the video is the format that fails there, measured twice.
The operator has already run all five networks simultaneously twice (09-11,
09-12) rather than phasing them in; treat that as the current default cadence
unless told otherwise.

## Ranking metrics, per channel

The launch plan ranks metrics globally: retention, then shares, then saves, then
real comments, with follower count and isolated likes deliberately ignored. That
ranking holds — but it is the _reach_ channels' ranking (YouTube, TikTok). On
the _traffic_ channels the ranking is clicks first: **Instagram**, now that its
bio link is the only measured converter, and **LinkedIn**, once its studio is
read again. Facebook has produced 0 conversations and no group-share activity
to date — it has not yet earned a traffic ranking; judge it on reach until a
group strategy exists.

Never compare a view across platforms. A YouTube Shorts view, a LinkedIn video
view and an Instagram Reels view count different things and are not commensurable.
